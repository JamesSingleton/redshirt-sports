How the poll works

The Redshirt Sports FCS Top 25 is voted on by a panel of 22 credentialed media members (example panel). Each voter submits a ranked ballot of 25 teams every week; points are added up and the published Top 25 is the order of total points.

22 voters

Invite-only panel, published every season

25-to-1 points

1st place = 25 points … 25th = 1

Every ballot public

Released with the poll, with outliers flagged

Points: 25 to 1

A team earns 26 minus its rank on each ballot: 25 points for a first-place vote, 24 for second, down to 1 point for 25th. A team left off a ballot gets 0 from that voter. With 22 voters the maximum is 550 points (22 × 25).

Ties share a rank (shown as “T-12”) and the next rank is skipped. Within a tie, teams are listed by first-place votes, then alphabetically. If teams tie for 25th, all of them are included.

First-place votes

The number in parentheses after a team, like North Dakota State (18), is how many voters ranked it No. 1. First-place votes add no bonus points beyond the 25 already counted; they’re shown because they show how decisive the top spot is.

“On X of 22 ballots”

Replaces the old “ballot share” bar. On 12 of 22 ballots means 12 of the 22 voters ranked that team somewhere from 1 to 25, and 10 left it off. It measures how broad a team's support is, which points alone can hide: a team on every ballot at No. 20 and a team on half the ballots at No. 8 can have similar totals.

Others, dropped out, no longer receiving votes

  • Others receiving votes: every team with points this week that isn't in the Top 25, by points.
  • Dropped out of the Top 25: teams ranked last week that aren't ranked this week, with last week's rank (they may still be receiving votes).
  • No longer receiving votes: teams that had points last week (ranked or not) and have none this week.

Eligibility

Voters
  • Invite-only; admins credential media who regularly cover the division
  • No athletics-department employees; team broadcasters only with disclosure
  • Conflicts (employer, alma mater, family, paid ties) disclosed publicly
  • One ballot per voter per week; MFA required on the voting app
  • Panel published each season; missed weeks are shown
Teams
  • Any program in the division per the published eligibility list
  • Programs transitioning between divisions follow the rule on this page for that season
  • Records are entered by admins; the poll stores no game data

Example policy text: final wording is set by Redshirt admins.

Ballot window and automation

The weekly cycle runs on schedules (FCS defaults shown; each poll has its own, configurable by admins):

  1. Sun 08:00 ET
    Window opens

    Automatic (cron). Voters get an email and push with their ballot link on vote.redshirtsports.com.

  2. Sun 18:00 ET
    Reminder 1

    Automatic, only to voters who haven't submitted.

  3. Mon 09:00 ET
    Reminder 2

    Automatic final reminder; admins see who is outstanding.

  4. Mon 12:00 ET
    Window closes

    Ballots lock automatically. Late games can extend the window; any extension is shown on the poll page.

  5. Mon 12:01 ET
    Tabulation

    Points, ranks, ties, movement, outlier flags and the CSV hash are computed automatically.

  6. Mon / Tue
    Publish

    An admin reviews the outlier report and publishes at the set hour. Ballots go public at the same moment.

Transparency

  • All ballots are public at the moment the poll publishes, and never before: the full grid, each voter's ballot and the voter roster.
  • Downloadable data: ballots CSV (voter, rank, team) and results CSV with a SHA-256 hash, so anyone can re-tabulate the poll.
  • Corrections log: any republish, void or window extension is listed on the poll page with the reason.
  • No early results: voters and staff can't see aggregates before publish.

Outlier policy

A pick is flagged as an outlier when a voter ranks a team 8 or more spots away from its published rank, or leaves off a team ranked in the top 15. Thresholds are configuration, not code.

  • Flags are informational: outliers are highlighted (gold ring) on the ballot pages, never altered.
  • Voters can add a short note to explain a pick; the note is shown with the ballot.
  • Admins review the outlier report before publishing, and look at repeated unexplained patterns at season end when renewing the panel.
  • Only invalid ballots are voided, with the reason published.

FAQ

Why can a team with fewer first-place votes rank higher?

Rank is decided by total points, not first-place votes. First-place votes are shown for context and only break display order when points tie.

What does “On 12 of 22 ballots” mean?

Twelve of the 22 voters put that team somewhere in their Top 25 this week; the other ten left it off. It's a measure of how broad a team's support is, separate from points.

Can I see how a specific voter ranked my team?

Yes. Every ballot is public at release: open Ballots for the grid of all voters, or By voter for each voter's full ballot with differences from the consensus.

Does an outlier ballot get thrown out?

No. Outliers are flagged so readers can see them, never changed or removed. Only invalid ballots (for example, a duplicate team) can be voided, and every void is published with its reason.

How do I become a voter?

There's no public application. Admins invite media who regularly cover the division; see Eligibility.

Why did the poll publish late this week?

If a late game forces a window extension, the poll page shows the new close time and the reason.
